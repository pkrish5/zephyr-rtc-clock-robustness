# Exploratory RTC delivery-delay pilot: feasibility stop (2026-10-06 UTC)

The user approved a separate nominal-clock pilot targeting 21 measured AI RTC suites with definite source-coded latency/ordering assumptions, a fixed-seed stratified sample of 10 of 23 unknowns, and all three upstream controls. The [prospective manifest](../results/rtc_latency_pilot/manifest.json) fixes those 34 IDs, four delay levels per suite (0, 1 ms, 10 ms, one RTC calendar tick = 1,000 ms), source/driver hashes, random seed 20261006, and three suite-disjoint worker partitions. The delays are **plausible exploratory probes, not datasheet-legal schedules**; they are excluded from RQ2 failure rates.

An isolated copy of Zephyr's STM32 RTC driver was linked into each variant in place of the original driver, with unchanged test C source and identical original Kconfig entries. The [build/config audit](../analysis/rtc_latency_build_config_audit.json) passed for **136/136** registered suite/level builds. An initial callback reference smoke at **1 ms** passed at nominal 32-kHz RTC input, with two measured ISR-delay intervals of **1,012.6 µs** each at 16 MHz.

The mandatory **one-tick reference smoke failed the feasibility gate**. Its [saved raw record](../results/rtc_latency_pilot/records/reference-rtc_alarm_callback-1000000us.json) and [UART trace](../results/rtc_latency_pilot/uart/reference-rtc_alarm_callback-1000000us.log) show one measured ISR delay of **1,000,010.7 µs**, only one delay marker where the reference expected two alarms, and a failure at the reference's `callbacks absent past legal-clock ceiling plus service margin` assertion after 26.013 virtual seconds. The [saved smoke audit](../results/rtc_latency_pilot/tick_smoke_audit.json) is `validated: false`. The injection holds the RTC interrupt handler in `k_busy_wait()` before setting pending and invoking the callback; a one-second hold can interfere with subsequent interrupt service. The trace establishes that the two-alarm reference behavior changed, but it cannot distinguish driver injection, Renode interrupt modeling, or firmware interaction as the cause. Keep that cause unknown.

Per the registered mechanism gate, **zero of the 136 pilot suite/level simulation verdict runs were launched**. The 136 successful builds are preparation, not outcomes. No pilot flip rate, model/context comparison, or silicon-latency claim is available. The original legal-clock records and test ELFs remain unchanged. The 1-ms and one-tick smoke records are retained; neither substitutes for a suite verdict. An amended pilot could drop the one-tick level or use deferred callback delivery outside the ISR, but would need a new frozen mechanism and reference validation before execution.

**2026-10-07 decision:** Defer dynamic latency injection to future work. A deferred-work or Renode delivery hook might avoid the long busy wait inside the ISR, but either changes the delivery path and requires a new registered driver/model implementation plus two-alarm reference validation. There is no demonstrated single alternative that can be validated within the requested one-hour budget. For this paper, retain the static latency-assumption category and its unknowns; do not treat the 136 builds as pilot outcomes.

Reproduce the read-only build/config check with `python3 analysis/rtc_latency_build_config_audit.py`. A fresh-process smoke replay would need a newly registered result namespace because the current runner preserves its immutable raw record on repeat invocation.
