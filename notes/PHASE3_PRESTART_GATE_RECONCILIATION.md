## Why both final counts are 321

The amended eligibility expression in `analysis/factorial_gate_summary.py` is `build pass AND nominal 5/5 AND functional == met`. The original strict expression adds `timing_bound == no_issue_found`. The audit recomputed both expressions independently for all 390 rows and found no mismatch.

| Five registered nominal passes | 321 |
| Build + 5/5 + functional `met` (amended primary) | 321 |
| Build + 5/5 + functional `met` + timing `no_issue_found` (original strict) | 175 |

The equality of 5/5 and amended-eligible counts follows from the screening order, not an omitted filter: only built suites labeled `functional=met` received all five nominal repeats. All 330 source-met suites built; 321/330 passed 5/5 and nine did not. Of the 60 source-not-met suites, 30 failed build. The other 30 received one nominal run: 12 passed, 17 failed an assertion, and one timed out. **No source-inadequate suite received a five-repeat screen, so its 5/5 outcome is unmeasured, not failed.** The functional filter rejects all 60. Among the 321 amended-eligible suites, 105 have `timing=inadequate`, 175 `no_issue_found`, and 41 `unknown`. Thus the strict gate excludes 146 additional runnable, functionally adequate suites.

The source reviewer treats a weak assertion that would let a plausible violation pass (for example, accepting a 30 ms wake for a requested 40 ms sleep) as a functional-oracle problem. An otherwise meaningful assertion whose chosen deadline can falsely fail under a legal schedule is a timing-bound problem. The vacuous delayed-work case below is functionally inadequate because its lower-bound expression becomes `elapsed >= 0` at 8,000 ticks/s; it also failed to compile. No source or raw result was changed by this audit.

## Estimated execution time after approval

The primary schedule has 100 seeds plus two separately reported endpoint challenges per suite. This gives **66 RTC/LSI suites × 102 = 6,732 fresh processes** and **255 HSI suites × 102 = 26,010**. Counts exclude replays of assertion failures and any additional upstream baseline runs.

The prior selected RTC seeded arm has 297 evaluable runs with mean **60.40 s** per process. Applying that mean to the new RTC count gives **112.9 serial machine-hours**. This is a rough transfer from RTC callback suites; calendar-counting and alarm-pending code can have different horizons. A planning range of roughly **90–160 serial hours** is prudent.

The prior 100-MIPS HSI arm has 4213 evaluable seeded runs. Weighting each task's measured mean by its new eligible-suite count gives **93.9 serial machine-hours** for 26,010 processes; use roughly **70–130 serial hours** for planning. The task-weighted figure is higher than an unweighted all-task average because the new suite mix contains more slow tasks.

With three independent Renode workers and ideal scaling, the base scans would take about **38 h RTC** and **31 h HSI** of wall time. Host contention, startup errors, logging, and replay make actual elapsed time longer. Each failing schedule requires a fresh exact replay, so add roughly one extra run's duration per failure: about 60 s per RTC failure or a task-dependent HSI duration. If all runs failed, replay could approximately double the base cost; the failure count is unknown before variation. Existing matched upstream runs cover the ten HSI cases and RTC callback; the two selected RTC state baselines may require fresh 100-seed runs under their locked schedules.

The preregistration fixes the inputs, eligible suite set, schedule order within each suite, and analyses, but does not require HSI to precede the new RTC arm. Running **RTC/LSI first** is compatible with it and surfaces the wide-tolerance signal sooner. Finish and preserve that arm before HSI; do not change either arm's eligibility, seeds, or endpoints in response to the first arm's outcomes. Start neither arm before user approval.
