# Additional wide-tolerance timing candidates — proposal, 2026-09-29

**Status update, 2026-09-29/30:** The user approved screening in the order below. RTC counting and alarm-pending each passed their own selected-case five-run nominal screens, with distinct frozen ELFs and registered 100-frequency LSI follow-on protocols. They are proposed adjacent state-observation tasks in the complete 13-task manifest; no varied-schedule result is claimed here. The separate IWDG [reset-state fidelity validation](IWDG_FIDELITY_RESULT.md) failed, so IWDG is excluded and is not being proposed as a qualified addition. The table below preserves the original pre-screen proposal and its selection rationale.

The original pre-screen proposal treated these as **candidates for user review**, not additions to the registered corpus. The pinned source is Zephyr v4.3.0 (`3568e1b6d5cdd51a6b964a2a1d6d29200fea2056`) on STM32F4 Discovery unless stated otherwise. At proposal time none had a registered seed set, selected-case nominal screen, frozen ELF, or 100-seed result. The STM32F407 LSI bound is 17–47 kHz at 3 V over −40 to 105 °C in [DS8626 Rev. 12, Table 35](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32f407vg.pdf); ST marks this characterization rather than a production test at every point. The same bound can govern different LSI-fed peripherals, but the observations are correlated by chip/clock source.

The existing [full-suite nominal RTC pilot](../results/phase1_lsi_rtc/uart_logs/rtc_api-nominal-000-mips16.log) reports `test_time_counting` and `test_alarm` passing at 32 kHz. That is a compatibility observation, not a selected-case eligibility screen under a newly registered protocol.

**Rejected for this board:** [`rtc_api.test_update_callback`](../subjects/upstream/zephyr/tests/drivers/rtc/rtc_api/src/test_update_callback.c) has a potentially sensitive 10 s callback-count window, but the pinned STM32 RTC driver does not implement `update_set_callback`. The selected case skips rather than measuring callback timing. Reconsider only with a separately modeled board and driver that actually supports update callbacks.

**Decision requested before corpus changes:** Prefer the RTC counting case as the lowest-risk next feasibility check, with the alarm-pending case as a second correlated RTC test. Treat IWDG as a separate model-validation spike before registration. None should be pooled with the pre-existing RTC callback result as independent evidence; adding them after inspecting source is outcome-aware selection and must be disclosed.
