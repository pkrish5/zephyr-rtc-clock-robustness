## 2026-10-06 quality-challenge limits

The early and late mutation operators change the modeled RTC to 64 and 10 kHz, outside the ST legal LSI range. They measure whether an otherwise functionally eligible test rejects specific faults, not its legal-schedule flip rate. The 1 Hz nonarrival clock operator crashed the peripheral model before any reference oracle and is excluded from scores. The replacement suppresses alarm delivery or freezes the returned calendar value in a separate driver build at nominal RTC frequency. This changes firmware driver code as intended by mutation testing; it cannot be mixed with frozen-binary clock perturbation results. Only reference-activated task-specific faults may enter mutation denominators. A two-fault interim score must be labeled as such if the replacement fails activation.

The replacement did activate on all three references and yielded 68 explicit kills in 68 evaluable suites. One original suite run ended in a Renode monitor startup error at virtual zero; it remains an unevaluable planned challenge, despite the other two mutant outcomes for that suite. Interrupted worker processes left three monitor/UART files without verdicts. These were preserved and hashed before 37 missing suite IDs ran in an append-only recovery namespace with the same registered source, binary, driver, model, and nominal clock. This recovery adds no independent fault operator or replication. The three selected mutants were chosen to probe particular oracle weaknesses; their 0/69 early, 52/69 late, and 68/68 evaluable nonarrival kills cannot estimate fault-detection performance over a broader defect population. The early mutant's zero kills, including upstream controls, indicates these particular tests lack the reference's external lower-bound check, but does not by itself show how they behave on physical silicon.

The approved latency pilot changes driver behavior in rebuilt test ELFs and may itself perturb interrupt nesting, observation order, and logger timing. A zero-delay variant and measured cycle deltas help separate injection effects from generic rebuild differences, but do not establish hardware realism. The 1 ms, 10 ms, and one-calendar-tick levels are plausible exploratory probes, **not datasheet-legal ISR latency bounds**. The 10 unknown suites are a fixed-seed, cell-stratified sample; their outcomes cannot be generalized to all 23 unknowns without sampling uncertainty. Any causal comparison with the 21 definite cases is descriptive because categories were coded after the clock-rate results and all tests share one driver, board, and simulator.

The 1,000-ms ISR busy-wait smoke measured the requested delay but lost or failed to deliver the second expected callback before the reference ceiling. This is a **mechanism validity failure**, not evidence that real STM32 delivery latency is one second or that the underlying upstream/AI suites are fragile. The pilot stopped before its 136 suite verdict runs. The missing callback's cause remains unknown among injection, model, and firmware interactions. The 136 passing variant builds cannot be counted as tests that passed or failed the delay challenge.

**2026-10-07 disposition:** Dynamic delivery-delay injection is deferred to future work. The one-tick hook executes `k_busy_wait()` inside the RTC ISR before setting pending and calling back, so it may itself disrupt interrupt service or Renode event handling. A deferred-work or model-level hook would change the intervention and requires new reference validation; no such alternative was demonstrated inside a one-hour engineering budget. The paper can report the static 21 definite and 23 unknown latency labels among 47 measured zero-clock-failure AI suites, stratified by prompt condition, but those labels are source judgments and have no dynamic pilot confirmation. The absent/present split (12/9 definite; 9/14 unknown) is post-start descriptive and task clustered, so it cannot isolate a tolerance-context effect.

The latency split also does **not** support a general substitution-effect claim. The historical bound-context `r05` immediate second-callback check is an illustration of one unsupported latency premise, outside the factorial denominators. The early/late mutation inputs are well outside the documented LSI range; their ideal arrival times probe assertion strength, not a chip-legal schedule or a physical fault-detection probability.

## 2026-10-05 latency-dimension amendment

The new latency-assumption label is a post-start secondary source-review category. It reuses the saved source evidence and does not change functional eligibility or the original timing-bound labels. Clock-rate variation alone cannot reliably evaluate asynchronous callback or thread delivery latency; even 0/100 clock-induced failures is not evidence that an exact-second or `K_NO_WAIT` callback check is adequate. The category's `not_found` result is absence of a definite unsupported premise in this review, not proof of full latency robustness; ambiguous finite service budgets remain unknown. The three RTC tasks and their generated suites are correlated through one board/driver/model.

The approved nominal-clock delay pilot uses modified driver binaries and deliberately injected delays. Its one-RTC-tick, 1-ms, and 10-ms levels are exploratory plausible magnitudes, not validated silicon latency limits or legal RQ2 schedules. Busy waiting in an ISR or first RTC read can affect interrupt nesting, cycle accounting, callback order, and the behavior under test; an achieved-delay trace and one-variant feasibility gate are required. An observed pilot flip would show sensitivity to this injection mechanism, not a measured physical-board failure rate. The 136-run pilot registration and isolated 1-ms callback smoke are recorded separately from the legal RTC arm.

## Phase 0 limits

- The Phase 0 firmware and both tests were deliberately authored for a mechanism check. Their flip rate says nothing about agent-written tests or human ztest suites. Those comparisons begin only after corpus selection and frozen generation.
- The Phase 0 sample is bare-metal STM32F4 firmware, not a Zephyr sample. Phase 1 has since built and run upstream Zephyr ztests in Renode; it does not retroactively turn the constructed Phase 0 result into evidence about upstream or agent test prevalence.
- Renode's `systickFrequency` is set to a hardware-supported HSI value, while `PerformanceInMips` remains an abstract 100 MIPS model. The timer-to-CPU relation and exception-entry latency are not cycle accurate. A physical board sample and, where possible, QEMU cross-check are needed before interpreting small boundary effects as hardware facts.
- The robust test's 12 ms deadline is a study design choice. The hardware datasheet bounds oscillator frequency at 25 °C; it does not guarantee end-to-end ISR-to-GPIO latency in this firmware. Claim only that the robust test survives the sampled legal clock values in Renode.
- The 1 µs Renode quantum is a simulator resolution setting. Changing it alone would not be a hardware-legal perturbation. The current flip comes from HSI frequency variation.
- One deterministic nominal schedule repeated 100 times is a sanity check for host noise, not 100 independent timing scenarios. Wilson intervals for perturbed seeds describe a prespecified uniform frequency sampling design, not a population of all embedded tests or boards.
- Renode's peripheral model may deviate from silicon. The four-way classification retains an artifact and an unknown category; a simulated flip is not automatically a test defect.

## Later-phase risks and mitigations

- **Subject selection:** Prefer boards with a physical counterpart and mature simulation. Record excluded subjects and reasons; do not generalize beyond modeled peripherals.
- **Existing-test selection:** A fixed sleep in upstream code is a candidate timing assumption, not proof of brittleness. Predefine inclusion by hardware feature, test objective, and simulator support; retain long waits and tests with no flips. Avoid selecting only tests whose constants sit near a perturbation boundary.
- **Board portability:** Documented Zephyr/Renode integration does not establish that a particular ztest and its GPIO or timer peripherals run on a particular board. Verify unchanged upstream suites on the selected board before counting them as the human baseline. Simulator timing issues require explicit artifact or unknown labels.
- **Legality:** Keep per-subject datasheet sources, environmental assumptions, and a proof that each perturbation lies inside the bound. Reject illegal schedules before looking at test outcomes.
- **Agent sampling:** Generate multiple suites per task with a fixed prompt/model version; retain compile failures and negative outcomes. Report both suite-level and test-level denominators.
- **Human comparison:** Match firmware functions, test framework, and stimulus contracts. Human tests are not a universal quality gold standard.
- **Authorship provenance:** Public Zephyr test files are an upstream-maintained baseline, not verified individual human authorship. Their commit history can establish provenance and age, but cannot rule out AI assistance; describe them as upstream tests unless authorship is documented.
- **Generation fairness and leakage:** Give agents the same written behavior and production-code context, but withhold upstream test files in the main arm. Keep a separate context-exposure arm if studying copying. Compare only tests that check the same intended behavior and report build, nominal-pass, and oracle-adequacy attrition.
- **Linked-test binaries:** Human and agent ztests linked into target firmware produce different full ELFs. Freeze each ELF across its timing schedules and compare unchanged production sources; never describe distinct linked-test ELFs as the same binary.
- **Clock-model coupling:** On the selected STM32F4 board, default Zephyr uses HSE/PLL; the study overlay selects HSI to justify its ±1% bound. Renode models SysTick frequency separately from integer CPU MIPS. The initial 16 MIPS pilot could not represent coupled ±1% throughput, so the registered Phase 3 HSI arm uses 100 MIPS with quantized 99–101 MIPS coupling. A positive timing flip that depends on this approximation needs artifact/unknown classification pending physical or cross-model validation.
- **RTC model and environmental range:** The new LSI arm changes Renode's RTC input frequency independently of the fixed CPU/SysTick model. The STM32F407 datasheet's 17–47 kHz range is stated for VDD 3 V and −40 to 105 °C; it does not establish that every sampled frequency occurs on one chip at room temperature. Renode's RTC prescaler, calendar, alarm, and IRQ routing may differ from silicon. A callback flip can support a model-specific timing-assumption finding after register-level diagnosis and replay, but a hardware claim still needs physical validation. No physical board was used in this extension.
- **Selected RTC configuration:** The RTC callback test is an unchanged upstream C file linked by a study wrapper that enables the optional RTC alarm and selects a supported seconds/minutes/hours alarm mask. This is one chosen configuration, not a random sample of upstream tests. Keep its rate separate from the ten Phase 1 HSI subjects, and do not infer the prevalence of human or agent timing assumptions from it.
- **RTC interval interpretation:** The extension's 7/30 Wilson interval describes repeated uniform draws of LSI frequency for one frozen case and one Renode model. Frequencies, not independently sampled tests or physical chips, are the sampling units. The 17 and 47 kHz endpoints are deliberate challenges outside that denominator. All eight observed failures have a model-specific brittle-test label because they share the same assertion and exact replay and two diagnostic continuations show delayed callbacks; a second simulator or silicon could change that attribution.
- **Perturbation sensitivity:** The Phase 1 HSI arm produced no eligible flips in 299 evaluable runs. This may reflect robust tests, generous waits, or a weak perturbation for these kernel API suites; the data alone cannot distinguish those explanations. A broader timing dimension needs a separately documented hardware bound and model validation before it can support RQ2.
- **Harness attrition:** One of 300 planned eligible runs failed during Renode startup at virtual time zero, then passed on fresh-process replay. Preserve it as unresolved in the original denominator; do not infer a test verdict from its replay.
- **Corpus diversity:** The Phase 1 candidates are multiple Zephyr ztest firmware images on one STM32F4 board, not multiple independent projects/boards. Estimate only this corpus's baseline and expand before broad embedded-system claims.
- **Repair comparison:** A longer sleep may lower observed flip rate by hiding timing variation. Compare against longer-wait and manual event-wait controls; require retained or improved mutation kills as well as assertions and report unrepairable cases.
- **Oracle strength:** Pair robustness metrics with assertion count/type and mutation score, so a repair cannot appear successful by deleting checks.
- **Classification:** Have reviewers apply the written decision procedure independently on a sample, retain disagreements/unknowns, and check selected cases across simulators and hardware.
- **Novelty:** The provisional combination claim could be narrowed by new workshop or preprint work. The literature watch in the notebook is required before paper submission.
